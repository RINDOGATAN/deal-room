## What it is and when it is used

Board minutes (in Spanish, *acta de consejo de administración*) are the written record of a meeting of the board of directors of a Spanish company. They record what the board decided, so that the company, its members and, where needed, the Commercial Registry have a reliable account of those decisions.

The Dealroom skill prepares the minutes under two Spanish texts:

- the **Ley de Sociedades de Capital**, articles 245 to 250, which govern how the board of directors is organised, called and adopts its resolutions; and
- the **Reglamento del Registro Mercantil**, articles 97 to 100, the Commercial Registry rules on minutes.

The skill also covers two situations that are common in practice: a **telematic session**, where directors take part in the meeting remotely, and **written resolutions without a meeting**, where the board decides in writing without meeting at all.

The skill is made up of 8 clauses.

## Who signs it

Board minutes are not a contract between two sides. They are a record kept by the company itself, about a decision of one of its bodies. Who must approve and sign them is set by Spanish company law and by the company's own rules, and the skill is built on the Ley de Sociedades de Capital and the Reglamento del Registro Mercantil for that reason.

For decisions of the members rather than the board, see [general meeting minutes (Spain)](/contracts/general-meeting-minutes-spain).

## Jurisdictions and languages

Dealroom offers these minutes for **Spain** only, and drafts them in **Spanish** only. This matches their purpose: they are a document of a Spanish company, kept under Spanish law. The page you are reading explains the document in English, but the minutes themselves are produced in Spanish.

Dealroom can be used alone, with one person completing the minutes, which suits a company record of this kind.
